03 · Personal Statement

Connecting technical work to human impact.

A personal statement about the experiences and ideas shaping my goals.

Science became most meaningful to me when it stopped feeling separate from the people it was supposed to serve. In class, I am interested in systems: biological pathways, mathematical models, and the technical decisions that make a design succeed or fail. Outside the classroom, my experiences in community health have shown me that even an elegant solution is limited if people cannot understand, access, or trust it. That connection between technical precision and human experience has become one of the clearest themes in the way I think about my future.

I am especially drawn to problems that require more than one way of thinking. Modeling glucose and insulin dynamics, for example, asks for mathematics and biology, but it also raises a larger question: how can quantitative tools help us understand health in ways that are actually useful? Studying engineering ethics creates a similar tension. A design can meet technical requirements while still demanding careful judgment about risk, responsibility, and the people who may be affected by it. I value work that requires both analysis and perspective.

Service has strengthened that interest. Working around community health care has made communication feel less like a secondary skill and more like part of the solution itself. Listening carefully, explaining information clearly, and noticing the barriers another person may be facing can change the usefulness of knowledge. These experiences have encouraged me to become someone who can move comfortably between technical detail and the broader context around it.

As I continue my education, I want to develop the scientific and analytical skills needed to solve difficult problems without losing sight of why those problems matter. My goal is not simply to understand complex systems. I want to use that understanding responsibly, communicate it clearly, and contribute to work that improves the way people experience health, technology, and the systems built around them.
