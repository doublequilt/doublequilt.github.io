What these pieces show together.

A short reflection on the experiences, skills, and values represented in this portfolio.

Looking across this portfolio, I see a consistent interest in connecting careful analysis with practical human concerns. My resume records experiences and responsibilities, while my personal statement explains the larger idea behind them: I am most engaged when technical knowledge has a clear relationship to people. The glucose–insulin modeling artifact reflects that pattern. It required quantitative reasoning, but it also reminded me that models are useful because of the questions they help us ask about real systems.

These experiences have also shaped the skills I value. I want to be able to analyze difficult problems, but I also want to communicate clearly, recognize uncertainty, and understand the consequences of a decision beyond the technical work itself. Service and academic projects have both pushed me toward that balance. Together, the pieces in this portfolio represent where I am now: still developing, but with a clearer sense that my future work should combine scientific thinking, responsibility, and attention to the people affected by the systems we build.
